The rules on every card

Red wire = power

5V on an Uno, 3.3V on an ESP32.

Black wire = ground (GND)

Every part needs a ground wire back to the board.

Other colours = signals

Each has a number that matches the checklist.

Breadboard strips

When many parts need power, they share the + and − strips.

🔌
Unplug first

Always unplug the USB cable before moving wires.

⚡
ESP32 = 3.3V only

5V can damage an ESP32, an RFID reader or a colour screen.

Parts guide

LEDA tiny light. The long leg is +. Always use a 220Ω resistor so it doesn't burn out.
RGB LEDThree lights (red, green, blue) in one. The longest leg is ground (−).
LED row10 LEDs in a line on the breadboard, each with its own 220Ω resistor.
Passive buzzerCan play music notes with tone(). Long leg is +.
Active buzzerBeeps one sound by itself when switched on. Long leg is +. Often has a sticker on top.
Push buttonConnects its legs while you press it. Use two legs on opposite corners.
ButtonsSeveral push buttons. One leg of each goes to a pin, the other legs all go to ground.
Sound sensorHears sound. DO says loud / quiet, AO gives a loudness number. Blue screw = sensitivity.
Distance sensorSends a sound you can't hear and times the echo, like a bat.
Light sensor (LDR)Changes with light. Paired with a 10kΩ resistor so the Uno can read a number.
Motion sensor (PIR)Notices warm bodies moving. Needs about 1 minute to wake up.
Temperature sensorMeasures temperature and humidity.
Servo motorTurns to an exact angle, 0° to 180°. Wires: brown = GND, red = 5V, orange = signal.
LCD screen (I2C)Shows 2 lines of 16 letters, using just 2 data wires (SDA, SCL).
IR receiverSees the invisible light from a TV remote.
RFID readerReads ID cards and key tags. Uses 3.3V only!
LED matrix64 red dots in an 8×8 grid for letters and pictures.
JoystickTwo sliders (X and Y) that give numbers, plus a click button.
Tilt sensorFeels which way it is tilted.
Colour screenA small 240×240 full-colour screen. 3.3V only.
